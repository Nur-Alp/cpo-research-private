# Celestica: CPO manufacturing-route dossier

**Owner:** Nur Alpys  
**Status:** Evidence-matched first-pass dossier; not an investment recommendation  
**Scope:** Switch-side CPO design/manufacturing route and possible profit-pool capture  
**As of:** 2026-08-07

## Current answer

**INFERENCE:** Celestica is now a credible manufacturing-route candidate in the switch-side CPO value chain, but not an evidenced optical-engine or profit-pool leader. Its Q1 2026 results disclose an awarded program to design and manufacture a CPO Ethernet switch for an unnamed hyperscaler, using 1.6T switch silicon, co-packaged optical interconnects and liquid cooling, with production ramp expected in 2027.[CMP-028][CLM-255][CLM-256] Its DS6000 announcement says a 64-port, 1.6TbE, up-to-102.4Tbps platform is available for order to initial customers and supports both copper and optical interconnects.[CMP-029][CLM-257]

The key boundary is important: an EMS/ODM design-and-manufacturing award may capture system assembly, integration, test, service and supply-chain value without owning the switch ASIC, PIC, laser, optical engine or CPO architecture. The public sources do not identify Celestica's exact scope, customer, unit volume, transfer prices, supplier share, yield or margin. The 2027 ramp is therefore a milestone to track, not a revenue forecast.[CLM-255][CLM-256]

Celestica's October 2024 disclosure provides earlier route context: it reported a major hyperscaler 1.6T switching program with revenue expected to ramp in 2026, but it did not call that program CPO. It must not be assumed to be the same program as the later CPO-specific disclosure.[CMP-030][CLM-258]

## What this changes in the company comparison

1. **Broadcom partner route:** Celestica strengthens the path from Broadcom TH6 silicon and CPO architecture to a system-manufacturing program, but it does not prove Broadcom TH6 CPO shipment volume.[CMP-018][CMP-028]
2. **Customer-proof ranking:** Celestica's disclosure is stronger than a generic collaborator quotation because it describes an awarded hyperscaler program and a planned production ramp. It is weaker than a customer-confirmed shipped SKU with units and repeat orders.[CLM-246][CLM-255][CLM-256]
3. **Profit-pool scope:** Celestica should be tested as a system-integration/ODM value-capture candidate, not automatically added to the PIC or laser supplier ranking.
4. **Alternative architecture control:** The DS6000 platform explicitly supports copper and optical interconnects. This creates a useful internal counterfactual: Celestica may capture system manufacturing value regardless of whether the customer selects CPO, optical pluggables or copper for a particular configuration.[CLM-257]
5. **Chronology control:** The earlier 1.6T hyperscaler program is logged separately from the later CPO program. This prevents a broader 1.6T switch revenue ramp from being counted as CPO revenue.[CMP-030][CLM-258]

## Falsification and next evidence

Reduce the importance of this route if the 2027 program is delayed, cancelled, uses only conventional pluggables/copper, or is immaterial to Celestica's economics. Upgrade it if Celestica identifies the customer/SKU, reports shipped CPO systems, discloses repeat volume, or shows that it owns a scarce optical-engine/package/test step with attractive margin.

The next evidence request is therefore not another broad CPO announcement. It is a product-level program record: customer, SKU, ports, CPO architecture, production date, Celestica scope, optical BOM, units, ASP, margin and capex.
