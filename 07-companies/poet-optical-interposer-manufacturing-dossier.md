# POET Optical Interposer manufacturing comparator

**Role:** Emerging optical-engine/PIC manufacturing comparator; not a current core-company leader claim
**Focus:** Wafer-level integration, optical-engine assembly/test and qualification-to-ramp conversion
**As of:** 2026-08-08

## What is now evidenced

| Dimension | Evidence-supported observation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Integration route | POET positions its Optical Interposer/EOI as wafer-level integration of photonic and electronic components intended to reduce active alignment and labour-intensive assembly | Company claim; no independent matched cost or yield study |
| Manufacturing footprint | POET says Globetronics and NationGate support Malaysian optical-engine assembly/test; a 10,000-square-foot Globetronics cleanroom has stated capacity of 1 million optical engines annually | Installed capacity is not production output, yield or utilization |
| Product/qualification signal | POET says an 800G receive engine was fully qualified and designed into several customer modules; 400G transmit and 1.6T chipset generations were sampled | Customer names, units, test boundary and field record are missing |
| Commercial intent | POET/Lumilens disclosed a $50M initial purchase order and potential $500M+ five-year framework, conditional on development, qualification and manufacturing scale-up | Order value is not recognized revenue and does not establish CPO units or margin |
| Ramp timing | POET/Lumilens expects late-2026 engineering samples and 2027 production aligned to hyperscaler deployments; POET/LITEON expects late-2026 prototypes and 2027 high-volume production | Management/partner targets, not observed shipment milestones |

## Investment interpretation

POET is relevant because it exposes the exact bridge this study needs to test: wafer-level integration may improve alignment, throughput and cost, but only a qualified engine with disclosed output, yield and realized economics can create a durable profit pool. The public record currently supports an **option-value manufacturing route**, not a technical, volume or profit-pool leadership conclusion.

## Required follow-up evidence

1. Production-lot yield and rework by engine generation.
2. Automated alignment/test cycle time and installed-line utilization.
3. Customer qualification reports, named SKU and shipped units.
4. Purchase-order conversion, ASP, gross margin and customer concentration.
5. Optical-loss distribution, reliability qualification and warranty allocation.
6. Whether POET, Lumilens, LITEON or the contract manufacturers own the final-engine BOM and process IP.
