## Shared rule

The `ANL-002` public-consensus snapshot is a consolidated scale check only. It cannot establish CPO revenue, margin, EPS or valuation sensitivity. Each card therefore separates **observed facts**, **external expectations**, and **Nur Alpys’ evidence-gated variant**. A positive strategic stance is not a positive stock call.

Use the [six-company commercial-proof queue](../six-company-commercial-proof-queue-2026-08-12.md) as the controlling next-action matrix. It keeps product, customer, scale and economics gates aligned across all six cards.

The [six-company card completion audit](six-company-card-completion-audit-2026-08-12.md)
is the controlling field-level check for the analyst/variant layer. It records
pending consolidated baselines separately from deliberately ineligible CPO
sensitivities.

The [six-company value-capture gate audit](../six-company-value-capture-gate-audit-2026-08-13.md)
is the cross-company reconciliation control. It identifies the one record that
would change each stance and prevents platform, component, process and
economic boundaries from being conflated.

## NVIDIA — positive strategic exposure; medium confidence

- **Evidence confidence:** Medium. Product and manufacturing-route disclosures are specific; a customer CPO numerator and economics are not.
- **Product boundary and observed facts:** Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics is NVIDIA’s 200G-SerDes switch-side CPO route. NVIDIA states it is in production and later in full production; its June source maps TSMC to SiPh fabrication, SPIL to package/assembly/test, TFC to laser validation and Foxconn to system assembly (`CLM-346`, `CLM-435`–`CLM-436`).
- **Customer / supplier evidence:** CoreWeave’s named SN6600-LD deployment is pluggable RHS, so it is not the needed customer CPO numerator (`CLM-380`–`CLM-384`). The ecosystem map is not an engine bill of materials.
- **Likely value capture (gated):** NVIDIA may retain platform/system value, but the record does not establish whether it captures, passes through, or shares PIC, engine, laser, package or service economics. No external-engine profit conclusion is permitted.
- **Expectation versus variant:** Management’s production language and named adopters imply a strong timing expectation. The variant is that platform deployment can occur before customer-confirmed CPO volume and before NVIDIA retains measurable CPO profit.
- **Earnings relevance:** CPO is not separately material within NVIDIA’s consolidated Data Center reporting (`CLM-088`). Direct CPO EPS sensitivity is **not eligible**.
- **Catalyst / falsification:** Upgrade with customer-confirmed CPO SKU, accepted units and repeat deployments. Downgrade if production remains unconnected to an identifiable customer CPO configuration or if service/yield pushes deployments back to modular architectures.

## Broadcom — positive enabling exposure; medium confidence

- **Evidence confidence:** Medium. The merchant CPO product boundary is unusually clear; accepted-customer and economic evidence is absent.
- **Product boundary and observed facts:** TH6-Davisson is Broadcom’s 102.4T merchant CPO switch definition: sixteen 6.4T DR engines, 200G links and field-replaceable ELSFP (`CLM-076`). The release contains both “now shipping” and early-access sampling language, so early-access sampling is the conservative maturity label (`CLM-077`).
- **Customer / supplier evidence:** HPE, Celestica, Micas and Nexthop establish partner-route evidence, not customer units (`CLM-246`–`CLM-249`). Celestica’s unnamed hyperscaler CPO program is planned to ramp in 2027, but it does not identify TH6, a customer, units or economics (`CLM-255`–`CLM-257`).
- **Likely value capture (gated):** Broadcom clearly controls the switch/SerDes product route, but the public record does not allocate engine, PIC/EIC, laser, package, test, warranty or system-margin value. Merchant-switch leadership is not proof of engine profit capture.
- **Expectation versus variant:** The architecture makes Broadcom the clearest disclosed merchant CPO route. The variant is that technical/product leadership can coexist with limited CPO volumes and low retained engine economics.
- **Earnings relevance:** Broadcom reports large AI-semiconductor revenue but no CPO revenue, margin or cash-flow line (`CLM-087`). Direct CPO EPS sensitivity is **not eligible**.
- **Catalyst / falsification:** Upgrade with a named TH6 customer, accepted CPO units and repeat shipment. Downgrade if sampling does not convert, 2027 program evidence fails to materialise, or LPO/NPO/pluggable systems meet the same need at lower qualified cost.

## Coherent — constructive component exposure; medium confidence

- **Evidence confidence:** Medium. The component and capacity routes are real, while product allocation and qualified volume remain unobserved.
- **Product boundary and observed facts:** Coherent demonstrates a 6.4T socketed SiPh CPO system, VCSEL CPO and 400G/lane InP-on-silicon paths (`CLM-068`). It also states that its first six-inch InP line is in volume production, without isolating CPO output (`CLM-069`).
- **Customer / supplier evidence:** NVIDIA’s completed $2B investment and access to five additional Coherent CPO-related product families are stronger route evidence than a generic partnership, but they do not disclose allocation, units or margin (`CLM-197`).
- **Likely value capture (gated):** Coherent could capture laser, PIC/engine or integration value if a named customer product allocates such content; the current evidence does not show which layer it supplies or whether yield/capex turns technology breadth into attractive margin.
- **Expectation versus variant:** Coherent presents a $15B+ 2030 CPO SAM and an H2 2026 CPO/NPO new-revenue milestone (`CLM-250`–`CLM-251`). The variant is that technology breadth and capacity only become valuable if a named product converts into qualified engine share and acceptable yield.
- **Earnings relevance:** Consolidated margin is reported but not CPO margin (`CLM-070`). Direct CPO EPS sensitivity is **not eligible**.
- **Catalyst / falsification:** Upgrade with a named NVIDIA/customer product allocation, qualified engine shipments and evidence of CPO-specific capacity utilisation. Downgrade if the H2 2026 milestone slips or if CPO content is won by competitors/platform owners at weak component margin.

## Lumentum — constructive/watch external-laser exposure; medium confidence

- **Evidence confidence:** Medium. The external-light route and order signal are material, but their boundary and economics are undisclosed.
- **Product boundary and observed facts:** Lumentum supplies an external-light/ELSFP route, with stated UHP laser performance and a 16-channel external-source demonstration (`CLM-071`–`CLM-072`). Its 2026 white paper reports a UHP laser-device accelerated-test programme with more than 100 million device-hours and zero reported catastrophic failures, plus a company 20-FIT rating at 400 mW (`CLM-553`–`CLM-555`). ELSFP proves a replaceable-light interface, not PIC/package serviceability; the accelerated result is not a field ELSFP-module or CPO-system result.
- **Customer / supplier evidence:** The company disclosed an incremental multi-hundred-million-dollar CPO order for delivery in H1 2027, but omitted customer, product, units, margin and cancellation terms (`CLM-083`). Its Q4 FY2026 release also identifies an initial ELS-module order and demand for ultra-high-power CPO lasers, again without customer, product, quantity or margin (`CLM-531`). NVIDIA’s completed investment and advanced-laser capacity agreement improve strategic route evidence but do not allocate CPO revenue (`CLM-198`).
- **Likely value capture (gated):** The most plausible exposure is external laser/light-source content rather than a whole engine. The order cannot be converted into content, gross profit or EPS until the product mix, supplier share, utilisation, warranty and pricing boundary are disclosed.
- **Expectation versus variant:** The order is the clearest near-term commercial conversion signal in the component layer. The variant is that its product/content boundary and realised margin may be much narrower than a full optical-engine thesis assumes.
- **Earnings relevance:** Q4 FY2026 reported gross margin is stronger at the consolidated company level, but remains non-allocable to CPO/ELS (`CLM-531`; `CLM-073`). Direct CPO EPS sensitivity is **not eligible**.
- **Catalyst / falsification:** Upgrade with order conversion, product/customer disclosure, qualified external-light content and margin evidence. Downgrade if delivery slips, economics are low-margin/capacity-heavy, or CPO systems choose integrated-laser or alternative supply routes.

## Marvell — strategic watch; low-to-medium confidence

- **Evidence confidence:** Low-to-medium. The technology route is identifiable, but the market/customer/financial conversion is still a management-target case.
- **Product boundary and observed facts:** Celestial’s Photonic Fabric is a 16Tb/s optical-I/O chiplet; it is an accelerator scale-up route, not evidence of switch-side 200G-lane CPO leadership (`CLM-094`).
- **Customer / supplier evidence:** Marvell forecasts meaningful revenue in H2 FY28, a $500M annualised Q4 FY28 run rate and $1B annualised Q4 FY29 run rate; these are management/transaction targets, not observed revenue (`CLM-095`). Its Q1 FY27 10-Q separately says Celestial revenue and earnings since the acquisition were not material—a current-period constraint, not a refutation of later targets (`CLM-551`).
- **Likely value capture (gated):** If Photonic Fabric qualifies in accelerator systems, Marvell could retain chiplet/interface value. There is no basis yet to determine content per system, product margin, yield burden, customer adoption or comparison with switch-CPO economics.
- **Expectation versus variant:** The market may value Photonic Fabric optionality before production evidence. The variant is that commercial timing, customers, margin and integration burden remain too unobserved for a CPO-led earnings call.
- **Earnings relevance:** Consolidated reporting includes Celestial but does not disclose Photonic Fabric revenue or margin (`CLM-096`); the more specific Q1 FY27 filing says the acquired business's revenue and earnings since acquisition were not material (`CLM-551`). Direct CPO EPS sensitivity is **not eligible**.
- **Catalyst / falsification:** Upgrade with named XPU/customer, production units and reported revenue/margin conversion. Downgrade if FY28/FY29 targets slip or a competing accelerator-optical-I/O architecture wins qualification.

## TSMC — strategic manufacturing-control watch; medium confidence

- **Evidence confidence:** Medium. The process route is directly evidenced; complete-engine/output economics are not.
- **Product boundary and observed facts:** TSMC describes COUPE as SiPh plus electrical-control-chip integration and reports 200G transmission with several customers; it separately targets/begins 2026 production milestones (`CLM-213`–`CLM-215`). The reported >99% figure is engineering-sample 3D-stacking yield, not final optical-engine yield (`CLM-216`).
- **Customer / supplier evidence:** Broadcom names COUPE technology-based optical engines in TH6-Davisson (`CLM-210`), but no source establishes whether TSMC supplies wafers, process technology, package services or complete engines.
- **Likely value capture (gated):** TSMC may control a critical SiPh/EIC-integration process step, but a process-control point does not prove it retains complete-engine value, final-package economics or CPO-specific foundry margin.
- **Expectation versus variant:** COUPE makes TSMC a plausible process-control beneficiary. The variant is that enabling a CPO stack may be economically immaterial or captured elsewhere without product-linked output and margin attribution.
- **Earnings relevance:** TSMC’s revenue, gross margin and capacity appropriations are consolidated and cannot be assigned to COUPE (`CLM-278`, `CLM-282`). Direct CPO EPS sensitivity is **not eligible**.
- **Catalyst / falsification:** Upgrade with a named COUPE customer SKU, qualified output and attributable package/engine economics. Downgrade if 2026 production milestones fail to translate into customer-qualified volume or if alternative integration routes bypass COUPE.

### What is complete versus what remains decision-critical

- **Complete enough for relative stances:** product boundaries, architecture
  class, public route evidence, evidence grade, catalyst and falsification
  condition are recorded for all six companies.
- **Not complete for an earnings call:** no company has the same-boundary
  customer denominator, accepted-unit/repeat record, supplier share, ASP,
  qualified yield/rework, warranty burden and product margin needed for a
  CPO-specific EPS or valuation bridge.
- **Current highest-value follow-ups:** exact `SN6800`/`SN6810-LD` customer
  acceptance; exact `BCM78919` acceptance; Lumentum order conversion; Coherent
  product allocation; and TSMC/Marvell qualified production output.

This audit prevents the cards from drifting from evidence-gated relative views
into unsupported company forecasts. It should be rerun whenever a source
changes a product boundary, customer record or economic input.
