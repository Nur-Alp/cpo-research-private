# CPO Decision Terminology

**Status:** Working definitions
**Last updated:** 2026-08-06

These definitions exist to prevent a demonstration, product announcement, or isolated customer trial from being mistaken for durable commercial adoption.

## Viability definitions

### Technically plausible

An architecture has demonstrated that it can meet the relevant performance requirements under a clearly defined test boundary. Evidence should cover bandwidth, reach, bit-error rate, link margin, all-in power, thermal behaviour, and the intended operating environment.

Technical plausibility does not establish manufacturability, reliability, serviceability, or acceptable economics.

### Manufacturable

The complete assembly can be produced with repeatable final-package yield, sufficient test coverage, manageable cycle time, scalable fibre attachment, qualified supply, and an economically acceptable scrap and rework profile.

### Production qualified

The product has completed the relevant supplier, system, and customer qualification requirements for its intended use. The exact tests, customers, and configuration must be recorded.

### Commercially viable

The architecture meets technical and operational requirements and is competitive with alternatives on total cost per delivered bit after accounting for:

- Host and module power
- Lasers and control electronics
- Cooling
- Yield loss and scrap
- Spares
- Repair and replacement
- Warranty cost
- Installation and maintenance
- Required capital expenditure

### Commercial proof

Working definition: repeat production orders or paid deployments from at least two independent customers, or one major customer with repeated production orders and credible evidence of sustained field use.

This threshold may be revised only with a dated explanation.

### Meaningful adoption

Working definition: at least 10 per cent adoption within a clearly defined addressable deployment domain, or revenue that is material to the relevant supplier, sustained or credibly contracted rather than represented by samples.

The denominator, period, network position, and evidence source must always be stated. The 10 per cent threshold is a research convention, not an industry fact.

## Commercial-stage language

| Stage | Minimum interpretation | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Announced | Company has described a planned product or architecture | Working hardware, qualification, or customer demand |
| Demonstrated | A prototype or system has operated under stated conditions | Manufacturability or field reliability |
| Sampling | Units are available to selected customers | Customer qualification or recurring orders |
| Qualifying | A customer or partner is testing the product | Successful completion or future volume |
| Limited production | Saleable units are being produced at constrained scale | Repeatable high-volume economics |
| Volume production | Repeat manufacturing at a defined production scale | Material revenue or broad adoption |
| Material revenue | Revenue is large enough to affect reported company results | Durable leadership or attractive valuation |

Never translate one stage into another without evidence.

## Leadership definitions

### Technical leader

The company with the strongest verified performance and architecture capability for a defined use case.

### Qualification leader

The company with the strongest evidence of completed customer and system qualification.

### Volume leader

The company with the greatest verified production shipments in a defined product category and period.

### Profit-pool leader

The company expected to capture the largest sustainable incremental gross profit after cannibalisation and execution costs.

### Platform leader

The company that controls system architecture, interfaces, ecosystem choices, or the customer purchasing decision.

### Best public-equity opportunity

The listed company with the most attractive probability-weighted return relative to expectations and valuation. This may not be the technical, volume, or profit-pool leader.

## Investment-research definitions

### Consensus

The adoption, revenue, margin, and valuation expectations reflected in available estimates, company commentary, investor positioning, and market price. Consensus must be evidenced rather than asserted.

### Variant perception

A specific, testable difference between the research view and evidenced market expectations.

### Catalyst

An observable event that may change probability, estimates, or valuation, such as qualification, production shipment, customer deployment, yield improvement, second sourcing, or a disclosed system metric.

### Disconfirming evidence

Evidence that reduces the probability of a hypothesis or invalidates an assumption. It must be recorded with the same discipline as supporting evidence.

### Thesis-changing evidence

Evidence strong enough to change adoption timing, probability, company ranking, or financial impact in the model.
