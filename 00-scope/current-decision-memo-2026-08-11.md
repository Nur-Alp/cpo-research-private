# Current CPO decision memo — 11 August 2026

**Status:** Private decision memo; not investment advice, a price target, or a publication-ready report  
**Evidence cut-off:** 14 August 2026; incorporates the newly retained PAP-051, PAP-055 and PAP-056 full papers  
**Decision:** Do current public records support a company-specific CPO investment conclusion?

## Answer

**No. CPO is not yet investable as a standalone public-equity thesis on the retained evidence.** The technical and product-route case has advanced far enough to define the right questions, but the commercial/economic chain remains unproven:

```text
exact CPO SKU → customer acceptance → units / ports → repeat shipment
→ supplier content / share → qualified good-engine cost → realised margin
→ earnings materiality and valuation gap
```

No named switch-side CPO deployment currently supplies that chain end-to-end. This is not a zero-probability conclusion; it is a discipline statement about the evidence required before treating a technology route as an investable profit pool.

## 14 August evidence update

Three full academic records strengthen technical and historical system-test confidence without clearing the commercial/economic chain. `PAP-055` documents historical TH5/Bailly CPO system testing, including more than one million 400G-port device-hours without uncorrectable codewords in a stressed self-loopback test; it is not TH6 deployment or customer fleet evidence. `PAP-056` adds measured 1.6T FOWLP package/coupling/interconnect evidence, but its 6.4T/12.8T scale-up and reliability/cost claims are modeled. `PAP-051` adds a measured 180-GBaud driver/modulator subassembly with explicit BER, temperature and partial-power boundaries, but no fibre-attached engine, reach, yield or qualification. The conclusion remains: **technology feasibility and production-route evidence are ahead of publicly demonstrated commercial scale and economics.**

## 2026–27 timing call

**Base reading:** 2026–27 is a *verification window*, not a volume forecast. NVIDIA and Broadcom product/maturity disclosures, Foxconn’s Q3 outlook, Celestica’s 2027 programme target, and Lumentum’s H1 2027 order window make it reasonable to monitor the period closely. They do not establish broad adoption or a profit-pool leader.

**Upgrade the timing call** only after a named operator or OEM ties `SN6810`/`SN6800` or TH6-Davisson/`BCM78919` to accepted units/ports and a repeat/expansion record.  
**Downgrade it** if product/production language continues to resolve to samples, unallocated platform deployments, or architectures that retain pluggables/LPO/NPO for qualification, serviceability or cost.

## The investable test

The research should not form a CPO-specific revenue, EPS, target-price or profit-pool conclusion until **all** conditions below are evidenced at the same product boundary:

1. named customer, exact SKU and accepted units/ports;
2. repeat shipment, expansion or renewal;
3. PIC/engine, EIC, laser, fibre attach, package, connector and test ownership/share;
4. final-engine yield, rework, qualification, service and warranty cost;
5. attributable ASP, product margin, price-down/cancellation and capital burden; and
6. a reconciled earnings-materiality and valuation-expectations bridge.

Until then, the operative conclusion is: **strongest switch-CPO timing signal; no proven CPO volume leader; no proven CPO profit-pool leader; no standalone equity conclusion.**

The unavailability of the missing fields is consistent with a technology that
is still early in commercialisation and with commercially sensitive customer,
yield and supplier-economics terms. This is a disclosure-context inference,
not evidence that CPO has no activity, nor evidence that it will succeed.
