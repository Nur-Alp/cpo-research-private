CPO investment brief

A concise, evidence-gated view of co-packaged optics adoption and value capture.
Author

Nur Alpys

2026 Q3 · Concise public brief · Evidence cut-off: 18 August 2026

Decision in brief

CPO is becoming a serious architecture decision at 200G/lane Ethernet scale-out, but it is not yet a proven public-equity profit pool. NVIDIA describes a CPO-based 200G-SerDes Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics system as in production. Broadcom has disclosed a third-generation 200G/lane CPO route and a 102.4T CPO path (NVIDIA 2026; Broadcom 2025, 2026). Those are meaningful product-route signals. The remaining investment work is to connect them to accepted units, repeat deployments, final-engine yield, service cost, supplier share and CPO-specific margins.

Inference: switch-side Ethernet scale-out is the leading near-term CPO domain. Forecast: 2026–2027 is the key commercial-proof window. Open question: no company is yet the evidenced profit-pool leader.

NoteWhat is not publicly available

The data required to choose a CPO volume or profit leader are not disclosed at a decision-quality product boundary: exact customer acceptance, unit/port denominators, repeat shipments, supplier share, realised content price, qualified yield/rework, field-service/warranty and product gross margin. Accordingly, this brief does not assume those data are zero; it concludes only that the public record cannot yet prove a leader or support a CPO-specific earnings call.

Question Current answer Confidence What would change it
Where does CPO matter first? High-bandwidth Ethernet scale-out switching Medium A named accelerator optical-I/O deployment with repeat accepted volumes
What is the timing call? Commercial proof must be tested in 2026–2027 Medium Named CPO SKU, accepted units and repeated shipment evidence
Does CPO automatically win? No. LPO, NPO and advanced pluggables remain live countercases Medium Matched system data on power, reach, service and cost
Who captures the value? Undetermined across platform, engine, laser, package and test layers Low Content, qualified share, yield and margin disclosures

What CPO changes

CPO shortens the high-speed electrical path between the switch ASIC and optical engine. That can relieve electrical-reach, power-density and faceplate constraints. It moves the hard problem inward: fibre attach, thermal integration, test, rework, qualification and serviceability become package/system concerns rather than a faceplate-module problem. OIF work supports the importance of this electrical/optical boundary, but it does not demonstrate that CPO wins total cost in a particular deployment (Optical Internetworking Forum 2023, 2024).

The decisive comparison is therefore not “CPO versus no optics.” It is CPO versus the best viable alternative at the same lane rate, reach and operational boundary:

Architecture Strength Principal risk Current investment read
Retimed pluggable Mature, modular replacement Power/density pressure at higher rates Baseline architecture
LPO Lower DSP/retimer burden while retaining a module boundary Demands host-channel and interoperability discipline Strong countercase where the channel holds
NPO Shorter electrical path with potentially better service boundary Product/interoperability model remains immature Important intermediate route
Switch-side CPO Shortest electrical path and dense integration Final yield, fibre attach and field service Strongest 200G commercial signal
Accelerator optical I/O Potentially powerful scale-up integration Distinct market, buyer and timing curve Strategic but separate opportunity

At 400G/lane, the technical pressure rises but the commercial conclusion does not become automatic. Public work on 400G/lane linear-drive systems remains model- and loss-boundary dependent rather than a matched deployed-system result (Zhou et al. 2026). The correct conclusion is that a short electrical boundary becomes more valuable as loss tightens; it is not that CPO has already won the economics.

The investable bottleneck

The best technology does not necessarily earn the best return. CPO value has to survive this chain:

Architecture need → qualified optical engine → supplier content and share → yield/rework/warranty → realised gross profit.

The weak links are almost entirely economic and operational. Public sources do not yet provide final-engine yield distributions, fibre-attach/test throughput, rework recovery, field-return data, warranty allocation, supplier content, ASP or CPO gross margin. A production announcement can establish product direction; it cannot establish a profitable CPO business.

This leaves four potential control points, none yet proven as the winner:

  • Platform integration: NVIDIA and Broadcom can shape architecture, qualification and customer route, without necessarily retaining optical-engine profit.
  • Optical engine/PIC design: Coherent and specialist silicon-photonics routes can hold valuable design and integration content, subject to qualified share and manufacturing cost.
  • External lasers: Lumentum and Coherent can benefit if external-light architectures become scarce, serviceable content rather than multi-sourced components.
  • Advanced package and test: TSMC and manufacturing partners can be essential if their integration flow is difficult to replace, but may also absorb capital, rework and warranty burden.

These are relative CPO exposures, not security recommendations or target prices. The broad company positions are grounded in public product and technology disclosures, but no current public record identifies CPO-specific earnings contribution for any of the six companies (Coherent 2026; Lumentum 2026; Marvell Technology 2026; TSMC 2026).

Timing, catalysts and disproof

The base case is not broad adoption; it is commercial proof: a clear CPO product boundary, named customer/accepted units, and repeat paid production. Meaningful adoption requires a larger system denominator and is not yet numerically defensible from public evidence.

Next observation Read-through
Named CPO SKU plus accepted units and repeat order Strengthens commercial timing, not supplier economics
Final-engine yield/rework and qualification data Tests whether deep integration is manufacturable
Field replacement, MTTR and warranty allocation Tests whether CPO can be operated economically
LPO/NPO/pluggable result with matched power, reach and service cost Directly weakens the need for CPO at that boundary
Supplier share, ASP and gross-margin disclosure Begins a credible profit-pool and earnings bridge

The thesis should be downgraded if CPO “production” resolves to evaluation quantities or non-CPO configurations; if qualified yield/service proves structurally poor; or if LPO, NPO or advanced pluggables meet comparable system constraints while retaining modular replacement.

Bottom line

The current edge is in correctly separating technical plausibility, commercial proof, and profit capture. CPO’s technical case is increasingly credible at 200G/lane switch-side Ethernet. Its adoption curve, manufacturing economics and public-equity winner remain unproven. The most valuable next source is not another market forecast; it is a customer/platform record that links a CPO SKU, accepted volumes, repetition, supplier boundary and qualified economics.
