## Executive-summary replacement

### The decision has become clearer, not more certain

**Inference:** switch-side CPO at 200G/lane remains the clearest disclosed
commercial route in Ethernet scale-out. NVIDIA has defined `SN6810` and
`SN6800` Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics CPO configurations; Broadcom has
defined the 102.4T, 200G/lane `BCM78919` / TH6-Davisson route. Those are
meaningful product and production-route signals. They do **not** by themselves
establish a public CPO revenue or profit-pool leader.

**Open question:** as of this edition, the public record does not join either
exact product boundary to all of: named customer, accepted unit or port count,
repeat shipment and attributable supplier economics. Consequently, the report
does not treat broad platform deployment, sampling language, ecosystem
partnerships or component-capacity announcements as proof of CPO-scale
adoption.

**Forecast / conditional timing view:** CPO becomes an investable scale-out
architecture only if it clears the same-boundary comparison: lower all-in inlet
power *and* repeatable qualified output *and* acceptable restored-port/service
cost relative to retimed optics, LPO and NPO. Better component pJ/bit alone is
not sufficient.

### What the new technical evidence changes

Three retained public technical records strengthen the manufacturing framework,
but not the commercial claim:

- A historical 51.2T CPO fabric-switch evaluation provides bounded system-test
  and stressed self-loopback context. It is not evidence of current TH6 or
  Spectrum-X deployment.
- A measured 1.6T FOWLP optical-package record makes package/interconnect
  feasibility more concrete. Its extrapolations to 6.4T and 12.8T must remain
  labelled as modelling rather than production yield evidence.
- A 180-GBaud driver/modulator subassembly result establishes a high-rate
  component boundary. It does not represent full-engine power, attach yield,
  qualification or field serviceability.

**Inference:** the current technical bottleneck is best described as a
qualified-good-engine chain—PIC/EIC, laser boundary, fibre attach, package,
test, burn-in, rework and field replacement—rather than as a single
"photonics" problem. The public record still cannot rank those steps by actual
production cost or margin.

## Methodology / change-log entry

**18 August 2026 — evidence refresh.** Added bounded public technical context
on CPO system testing, FOWLP packaging and 180-GBaud component operation.
Refreshed exact-SKU commercial routes for NVIDIA and Broadcom. No publicly
verifiable record was found that clears the named-customer, accepted-unit,
repeat-shipment or supplier-economics gates for `SN6810`, `SN6800` or
`BCM78919`. The timing view and company stances are unchanged; confidence in
the distinction between product-route evidence and commercial proof is higher.
