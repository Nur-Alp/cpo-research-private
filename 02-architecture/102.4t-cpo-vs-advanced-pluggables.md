# 102.4T Switch-Side CPO Versus Advanced Pluggable Optics

**Research question:** Does a 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet switch require co-packaged optics, or can 200G-per-lane linear and retimed pluggable optics remain commercially viable?

**Status:** Preliminary evidence review; academic LPO boundary updated

**As of:** 2026-08-07

## Preliminary answer

**INFERENCE:** Aggregate switch capacity alone does not make CPO technically necessary at 102.4 Tb/s. Broadcom documents a 102.4T CPO implementation, while Arista reports a live 102.4T demonstration supporting 1.6T linear, linear-receive, and fully retimed pluggable optics.[1][2] This establishes at least two technically plausible integration paths at the same aggregate switch bandwidth.

The public evidence does not yet establish which path has better all-in commercial economics. Broadcom publishes strong power, cost, and reliability claims for CPO, but the reviewed materials do not provide a controlled 102.4T comparison with equivalent pluggable systems. Arista demonstrates pluggable feasibility but does not disclose system power, thermal headroom, production yield, qualification status, field reliability, or total cost for the demonstrated configuration.[1][2]

NVIDIA's own technical material provides a useful vendor boundary but not a controlled comparison: it illustrates up to 22 dB electrical loss for a 200G pluggable path, approximately 4 dB for its CPO path and CPO power as low as 9W per interface.[12] These figures should be kept alongside, not substituted for, the measured and modeled academic LPO evidence. NVIDIA also lists Spectrum-X and Quantum-X CPO product boundaries and an ecosystem of technology partners, but does not map partner responsibility or disclose final-engine economics.[13]

The decision should therefore be framed by deployment domain. CPO is more likely to win where optical attach rate, bandwidth density, link stability, and energy constraints outweigh the value of field-replaceable modules and multi-vendor supply. Advanced pluggables remain credible where modularity, serviceability, procurement flexibility, and deployment familiarity have greater value.

The academic LPO evidence adds an electrical-boundary condition to this conclusion. Meta directly measured 100G/lane LPO in a 51.2T switch, but its 200G result is modeled and requires roughly 22-23 dB loss for the stated linear-drive limits; its approximately 30 dB planned cabled path needs a transmit retimer. Separate Hisense and Semtech models report 31 dB and 26 dB cases, but their device, equalisation, return-loss and FEC assumptions differ materially. This is conditional technical feasibility, not proof of a commercially qualified 200G pluggable system.[8][9][10] At 400G/lane, the reviewed evidence directly measures components at 160-180 GBd and models 212.5 GBd only up to 12 dB B2B loss, which presently favours moving the electrical boundary inward toward NPO/CPO rather than proving a conventional 400G LPO module.[11]

### What the 400G and COUPE milestones change

The emerging 400G/lane standards work does not eliminate the architectural countercase. IEEE P802.3dj's current 400GPL objectives include optional single-lane 400G electrical interfaces for chip-to-module and chip-to-chip use, a 500 m single-mode-fibre objective, and a 1 m twinaxial-cable objective.[14] These are project objectives, not an adopted channel-loss budget or evidence that a production 400G LPO module has been qualified. The correct interpretation is that 400G/lane keeps multiple electrical-boundary choices open while loss, return-loss, FEC, thermal and service requirements are negotiated.

TSMC's COUPE-on-substrate announcement provides a separate manufacturing checkpoint: TSMC says true CPO begins production in 2026 and identifies a 200G-per-channel microring-modulator implementation.[15] TSMC's platform material reports greater than 99% 3D-stacking yield on engineering samples, while its annual-report disclosure says 200G transmission was demonstrated with several customers and that volume production was targeted for 2026.[16][17] These records strengthen the plausibility of an integrated PIC/EIC and 3D-stacking route, but they do not show final optical-engine yield, fibre-attach loss distribution, named production SKUs, shipped units, or an all-in CPO-versus-pluggable cost advantage. COUPE therefore raises the credibility of an NPO/CPO manufacturing path; it does not make CPO mandatory for a 102.4T switch or establish TSMC as the complete-engine profit leader.

**Decision implication:** retain at least two live 102.4T routes in the model—(1) advanced pluggables with an external electrical boundary and (2) NPO/CPO with the boundary moved into the package. Treat 400GPL and COUPE as qualification and manufacturing gates, not as adoption outcomes. The architecture call should change only when matched channel data, final-engine yield, field-service evidence and customer production records close the current gaps.

## What the evidence supports

1. **FACT:** Industry standards activity supports 200G-per-lane Ethernet and multiple energy-efficient interface architectures.[3][6]
2. **COMPANY CLAIM:** Broadcom has a specified 102.4T CPO device with 512 optical lanes and integrated optical engines.[1]
3. **COMPANY CLAIM:** Arista has demonstrated a 102.4T switch supporting several advanced pluggable architectures.[2]
4. **INFERENCE:** A 102.4T switch does not inherently require CPO. The economic winner depends on the system configuration and operating requirements.
5. **UNKNOWN:** No reviewed source supplies a like-for-like, independently verified comparison of total system power, cooling, acquisition cost, failure rate, repair cost, and deployment labour.

## Provisional decision

Do not make a universal 102.4T architecture call yet. Use the following conditional view:

- **CPO leads provisionally** for highly optically attached AI back-end switches where power density and link stability are binding and the operator accepts an integrated service model.
- **Advanced pluggables lead provisionally** where optical ports must be field replaceable, multiple vendors are required, attach rates vary, or deployment flexibility has high economic value.

At 200G/lane, require a measured channel-specific margin before treating LPO as the leading pluggable option; beyond that qualified loss and return-loss boundary, compare a retimed pluggable with NPO/CPO rather than assuming linear drive will close. At 400G/lane, do not classify conventional LPO as technically established.

Confidence is **medium** that both architectures are technically plausible at 102.4T and **low** on which is commercially superior. There is not yet enough public evidence to assign a meaningful-adoption year or a profit-pool leader.

## Initial power model

The first matched-boundary scenario model is available at [`08-model/102.4t-switch-side-power-model.md`](../08-model/102.4t-switch-side-power-model.md).

Its central scenario produces approximately 601 W for CPO, 668 W for LPO, and 2,004 W for fully retimed optics after illustrative power-conversion and incremental cooling overhead. These figures are derived scenarios, not measured chassis results. The model indicates a potentially large benefit from removing full DSP retiming, but only a 10% central CPO advantage over LPO. That narrower difference could be reversed by omitted CPO-specific laser, control, thermal, or service overhead.

## Evidence needed next

1. Matched 102.4T chassis power at equal port count, reach, workload, cooling boundary, and error-rate target
2. Module and system cost, including external lasers, fibres, connectors, cooling, spares, and installation
3. Final-package yield, rework rate, and test time for CPO
4. Field failure and link-flap rates for both architectures
5. Repair procedure and replacement cost for a failed co-packaged engine
6. Named customer qualification and repeat production deployment
7. Multi-vendor availability and practical interoperability
8. Evidence on whether pluggable thermal density constrains a commercially realistic 102.4T faceplate
9. Matched 200G-per-lane multi-vendor system data at a stated electrical-loss, return-loss, FEC, reach and temperature boundary
10. A measured 212.5-GBd/400G-per-lane link before treating 400G LPO as feasible
