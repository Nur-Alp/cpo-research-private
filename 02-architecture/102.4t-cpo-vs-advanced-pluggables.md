# 102.4T Switch-Side CPO Versus Advanced Pluggable Optics

**Research question:** Does a 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet switch require co-packaged optics, or can 200G-per-lane linear and retimed pluggable optics remain commercially viable?

**Status:** Preliminary evidence review

**As of:** 2026-08-06

## Preliminary answer

**INFERENCE:** Aggregate switch capacity alone does not make CPO technically necessary at 102.4 Tb/s. Broadcom documents a 102.4T CPO implementation, while Arista reports a live 102.4T demonstration supporting 1.6T linear, linear-receive, and fully retimed pluggable optics.[1][2] This establishes at least two technically plausible integration paths at the same aggregate switch bandwidth.

The public evidence does not yet establish which path has better all-in commercial economics. Broadcom publishes strong power, cost, and reliability claims for CPO, but the reviewed materials do not provide a controlled 102.4T comparison with equivalent pluggable systems. Arista demonstrates pluggable feasibility but does not disclose system power, thermal headroom, production yield, qualification status, field reliability, or total cost for the demonstrated configuration.[1][2]

The decision should therefore be framed by deployment domain. CPO is more likely to win where optical attach rate, bandwidth density, link stability, and energy constraints outweigh the value of field-replaceable modules and multi-vendor supply. Advanced pluggables remain credible where modularity, serviceability, procurement flexibility, and deployment familiarity have greater value.

## Evidence comparison

| Dimension | Broadcom TH6-Davisson CPO | Arista advanced pluggable path | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch capacity | **COMPANY CLAIM:** BCM78919 supports 102.4 Tb/s and up to 64 x 1.6TbE or 128 x 800GbE ports.[1] | **COMPANY CLAIM:** Arista showed a 102.4 Tb/s, 4RU, 3 nm switch demonstration.[2] | Both paths have been demonstrated or documented at 102.4T. |
| Host interface | **COMPANY CLAIM:** 512 x 200G PAM4 all-optical I/O, directly driven from the switch SerDes.[1] | **COMPANY CLAIM:** 200G-per-lane 1.6T LPO, LRO, and FRO, plus gearbox-based 800G optics.[2] | Both target 200G-per-lane generation interfaces. |
| Electrical path | Co-packaging shortens the high-speed electrical path and removes the faceplate module electrical connector.[1][3] | The signal must traverse a chip-to-module channel; LPO removes or reduces module retiming, while FRO retains it.[2][3] | CPO has the structural signal-integrity and interface-power advantage. The magnitude is unverified here. |
| Power | **COMPANY CLAIM:** Broadcom describes CPO as dramatically reducing system power and providing the lowest-power optical-interconnect solution.[1] | **UNKNOWN:** No comparable system-power figure is disclosed for Arista's 102.4T demonstration.[2] | No controlled comparison is available. Do not use vendor percentages as a conclusion. |
| Serviceability | **COMPANY CLAIM:** TH6-Davisson uses field-replaceable external-laser modules.[4] The reviewed brief does not establish field replacement of a failed co-packaged optical engine.[1] | Faceplate pluggables are replaceable during service. OIF identifies interchangeability and in-service replacement as core advantages.[3] | Pluggables retain the clearer service model. External lasers mitigate only part of CPO's repair problem. |
| Supply chain | Broadcom integrates the switch, SerDes, and optical engines. Its product catalogue classifies BCM78919 as limited release.[4][5] | OIF identifies the mature fabrication, testing, and multi-vendor interchangeability of faceplate pluggables as advantages.[3] | Pluggables currently have the stronger supply-chain structure. CPO may trade sourcing flexibility for integration benefits. |
| Interoperability | **COMPANY CLAIM:** Broadcom says Davisson can interconnect with DR transceivers, LPO, and CPO links at 200G per channel.[4] | **COMPANY CLAIM:** Arista reports interoperability among 1.6T advanced pluggables and gearbox-based 800G optics.[2] | Link interoperability does not prove that internal CPO engines or pluggable modules are multi-sourced. |
| Commercial stage | Broadcom's announcement says “shipping”, its availability section says sampling to early-access customers, and its catalogue says limited release.[4][5] | Arista describes a live demonstration, not a qualified production deployment.[2] | Neither source set proves broad, repeat production deployment at 102.4T. |
| Standards | IEEE P802.3dj is developing Ethernet specifications using 200 Gb/s signalling per lane.[6] OIF has published requirements and co-packaging agreements.[3][7] | The same standards work supports pluggable, linear, near-package, and co-packaged implementation choices.[3][6] | Standards enable comparison and interoperability but do not select an economic winner. |

## What the evidence supports

1. **FACT:** Industry standards activity supports 200G-per-lane Ethernet and multiple energy-efficient interface architectures.[3][6]
2. **COMPANY CLAIM:** Broadcom has a specified 102.4T CPO device with 512 optical lanes and integrated optical engines.[1]
3. **COMPANY CLAIM:** Arista has demonstrated a 102.4T switch supporting several advanced pluggable architectures.[2]
4. **INFERENCE:** A 102.4T switch does not inherently require CPO. The economic winner depends on the system configuration and operating requirements.
5. **UNKNOWN:** No reviewed source supplies a like-for-like, independently verified comparison of total system power, cooling, acquisition cost, failure rate, repair cost, and deployment labour.

## Provisional decision

Do not make a universal 102.4T architecture call yet. Use the following conditional view:

- **CPO leads provisionally** for highly optically attached AI back-end switches where power density and link stability are binding and the operator accepts an integrated service model.
- **Advanced pluggables lead provisionally** where optical ports must be field replaceable, multiple vendors are required, attach rates vary, or deployment flexibility has high economic value.

Confidence is **medium** that both architectures are technically plausible at 102.4T and **low** on which is commercially superior. There is not yet enough public evidence to assign a meaningful-adoption year or a profit-pool leader.

## Evidence needed next

1. Matched 102.4T chassis power at equal port count, reach, workload, cooling boundary, and error-rate target
2. Module and system cost, including external lasers, fibres, connectors, cooling, spares, and installation
3. Final-package yield, rework rate, and test time for CPO
4. Field failure and link-flap rates for both architectures
5. Repair procedure and replacement cost for a failed co-packaged engine
6. Named customer qualification and repeat production deployment
7. Multi-vendor availability and practical interoperability
8. Evidence on whether pluggable thermal density constrains a commercially realistic 102.4T faceplate
