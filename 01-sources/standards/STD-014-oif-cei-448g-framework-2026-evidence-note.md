## What this source establishes

OIF distinguishes four physical electrical-attachment contexts at 448G: a
die-to-on-package path for CPO, a die-to-near-package path for NPO, a
die-to-module path, and a chip-to-chip path. It states that interoperability
across these contexts requires electrical specifications, mechanical
compatibility, management interfaces, multi-vendor interoperability testing,
and reference/compliance tools.[CLM-563]

## Decision use

This is a control for the alternatives comparison: NPO is not simply a synonym
for CPO, because it retains a distinct physical interface boundary. Nor does a
short electrical path, by itself, prove interoperability or a service and
economic advantage.

## Evidence boundary

This is an OIF framework, not a ratified product-interface agreement, a
customer qualification report, or a matched NPO/CPO system test. It provides
no customer SKU, BER distribution, field-service procedure, accepted-unit
denominator, yield, rework, warranty, ASP, margin, or cost-per-restored-port
result. It must not be used to establish that NPO or CPO has won commercially.
