## Scope decision

The article’s Groq/LPU, inference scheduling, memory, and rack-system analysis is broader than the active optical-engine thesis. Only its CPO roadmap and optical-domain implications are retained here.

## Evidence that can be used

The article presents a coherent roadmap hypothesis:

1. Rubin and Rubin Ultra systems remain copper inside the rack, while CPO is introduced for larger inter-rack scale-up domains such as NVL576 and later NVL1152.
2. The first larger-world-size CPO deployment is expected to be a limited-volume or “test” step, with higher-volume CPO dependent on manufacturing, cost, reliability, and supply-chain learning.
3. NVIDIA’s “copper where it can, optics where it must” approach keeps switch-side CPO, accelerator-side optical I/O, and inter-rack scale-up CPO as separate adoption cases.
4. Roadmap economics should be tested against the optical-engine/ELS, fibre, switch, service, and system-level profit boundaries rather than against a generic CPO TAM.

## Claims deliberately not accepted as facts

The article contains many analyst estimates about NVIDIA’s systems, Groq transaction structure, product timing, supplier relationships, and future rack configurations. Those are not treated as shipments, customer commitments, production volumes, or confirmed CPO architecture without NVIDIA primary material or observed deployment.

## Research actions created

- Add inter-rack scale-up CPO as a separate row in the adoption timeline.
- Track NVL576/NVL1152 as roadmap milestones with low-confidence timing and explicit copper-within-rack versus CPO-between-rack boundaries.
- Keep NVIDIA’s platform control and customer route separate from optical-engine manufacturing leadership.
- Recover and retain the cited NVIDIA technical blog and primary roadmap/financial-analyst material.
