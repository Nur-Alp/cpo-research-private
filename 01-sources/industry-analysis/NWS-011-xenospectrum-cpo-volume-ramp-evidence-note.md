# NWS-011 — XenoSpectrum: CPO volume-ramp triangulation

**Canonical source:** <https://xenospectrum.com/en/nvidia-broadcom-cpo-volume-ramp/>  
**Publisher:** XenoSpectrum  
**Publication date:** 2026-07-27  
**Local retention:** The publisher blocked direct archival download in this environment; the canonical link is retained.

## Evidence reported

The article, which says it is translated from a Japanese original and cites TrendForce among other sources, reports:

- NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics and Broadcom Bailly shipments to some partners/customers in limited volumes as of July 2026.
- It could not determine whether those destinations represented evaluation/verification or commercial deployment.
- Shipment volumes, yield rates and general-availability timing were not publicly disclosed.
- It describes the supply chain as involving TSMC COUPE stacking, SPIL package assembly/testing, TFC Communication laser-die modularization/reliability verification and Foxconn system assembly.
- It repeats a TrendForce estimate of approximately 0.5% CPO share of AI-data-centre optical modules in 2026 and potentially 35% by 2030; it also repeats market-size estimates for combined CPO/NPO and pluggables.

## Correct use

This is secondary triangulation, not customer proof. It supports keeping the “in production” versus “widely available” distinction and prioritizing yield/capacity evidence. The supply-chain role map and market percentages remain reported estimates until the cited primary records are obtained and reconciled.

## Limitations

- Secondary publication; translated article; underlying TrendForce reports were not retained here.
- No named customer SKU, unit count, qualification lot, final-engine yield, ASP, margin or field data.
- Market-share and market-size figures are not used as base-case model inputs.

**Use:** secondary timing and supply-chain triangulation only. See `CLM-411`–`CLM-415`.
