# FIL-016 — Aehr FY2026 results: silicon-photonics burn-in demand boundary

**Publisher:** Aehr Test Systems  
**Published:** 14 July 2026  
**Accessed:** 13 August 2026  
**Source type:** Official company FY2026 results release  
**Canonical source:** https://www.aehr.com/2026/07/aehr-test-systems-reports-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-financial-results-with-record-quarterly-bookings-and-100-million-effective-backlog/

## What it adds

Aehr says its lead silicon-photonics customer is ramping and had placed
follow-on orders for fully automated wafer-level burn-in systems serving AI
optical I/O and data-center interconnects. It also says a newer major
silicon-photonics customer, described as a global networking-products and
solutions leader, provided a forecast for additional systems while ramping
capacity for next-generation hyperscale deployments.

This is a material manufacturing **demand/capacity** signal: it is stronger
than a test-equipment capability announcement because it refers to a customer
ramp and follow-on production orders.

## Boundary

The release does not identify either customer, an NVIDIA or Broadcom SKU,
whether the application is switch CPO rather than a transceiver or accelerator
optical-I/O route, wafer starts, test duration, coverage, pass/fail counts,
yield, rework, installed utilisation, accepted output, field returns, test
cost or supplier economics.

It cannot be combined with the other Aehr orders to derive CPO units, good-die
output, a test-cost curve, a yield improvement, or a supplier revenue/margin
allocation.

## Controlled use

Use as evidence that production-scale wafer-level reliability screening is a
live silicon-photonics manufacturing requirement. Keep every product-matched
yield, service and profit-pool input blocked.
