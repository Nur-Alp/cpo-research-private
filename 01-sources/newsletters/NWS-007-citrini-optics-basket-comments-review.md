## Why it is retained

The post comments on an optics-company basket involving Aixtron, Furukawa, Nokia, Soitec, AAOI, Himax, and POET. Most of its content is opinion about tickers and portfolio weights, outside the active technical thesis. The useful portion is a reminder that optical-engine claims need coupling, polarization, alignment, loss, laser-noise, and qualified-yield evidence.

## Evidence that can be used

1. Furukawa connector and ferrule capabilities should be separated from proof of an ultra-high-power external-laser product; the repository already retains PAP-018 and PAP-026 for the connector/splitter boundaries.
2. Nokia/InP or other six-inch capacity claims should be tested with die-bin yield, qualified 200G output, and customer acceptance—not wafer capacity alone.
3. Any Himax/POET optical-attach claim needs coupling efficiency, polarization-extinction ratio, alignment tolerance, insertion loss, RIN, linewidth, and scale/yield data. PAP-014 provides direct POET interposer measurements but not those commercial gates.

## Claims deliberately not accepted as facts

The post's ticker rankings, valuation statements, claims that a company is “vaporware,” and forecasts about NVIDIA CPO timing or Celestial sourcing are market opinion. Its criticisms of Furukawa, Himax, POET, AAOI, or Coherent are not independent product or customer evidence.

## Research actions created

- Add coupling efficiency, polarization extinction, alignment tolerance, and laser-noise fields to attach/engine diligence.
- Keep Furukawa connector/splitter evidence separate from high-power-laser leadership.
- Keep POET's measured interposer results in the packaging benchmark, but do not infer Celestial/Marvell revenue or production adoption.
