# PAP-034 — Low-Loss Integration of High-Density Polymer Waveguides with Silicon Photonics for CPO

- **Authors:** Jef Van Asch et al.
- **Affiliations:** imec / Ghent University; Huawei (one author)
- **Canonical paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02712
- **Retained PDF:** `PAP-034-van-asch-polymer-waveguides-cpo-2025.pdf`
- **Publication:** 2025-03-04 (arXiv)

## Evidence extracted

The paper experimentally evaluates two SiN-to-polymer optical redistribution-layer routes: lithographically patterned polymer waveguides on the PIC and a flip-chip bonded polymer-waveguide substrate. For the lithography route, five functional samples were measured. At 1310 nm the reported adiabatic coupling loss was −0.56 ± 0.37 dB (TE) and −1.04 ± 0.42 dB (TM), with sub-2 dB chip-to-chip coupling under the stated short-ORDL path. Polymer propagation loss was 0.43 ± 0.15 dB/cm (TE) and 0.48 ± 0.30 dB/cm (TM); the ORDL pitch was 50 µm.

The flip-chip route was materially less reproducible: 16 samples were fabricated in three batches, but only two showed acceptable functional taper results. The authors attribute failures mainly to warpage and report that a 1 µm vertical offset can reduce coupling efficiency by about 10 dB in simulation.

## Evidence boundary

This is unusually useful yield/reproducibility evidence for a CPO interface, but it is not a qualified engine lot. The lithography sample count is five; the flip-chip result is a small process experiment with a 12.5% acceptable-sample fraction under that specific test setup, not a production yield estimate. There is no complete engine, thermal cycling, lifetime, customer qualification, ASP or margin evidence.
