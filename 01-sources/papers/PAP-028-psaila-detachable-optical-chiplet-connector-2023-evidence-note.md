# PAP-028 — Psaila et al., detachable optical chiplet connector

**Citation:** Nicholas Psaila, Srikant Nekkanty, David Shia and Pooya Tadayon, “Detachable Optical Chiplet Connector for Co-Packaged Photonics,” *Journal of Lightwave Technology* 41(19), 6315–6323 (2023). DOI [10.1109/JLT.2023.3285149](https://doi.org/10.1109/JLT.2023.3285149). [Publisher record](https://opg.optica.org/jlt/abstract.cfm?uri=jlt-41-19-6315)

**Archive note:** the same-ID local `.html` file is a retained Optica/Radware CAPTCHA access attempt, not article text. The full PDF is retained separately and is the evidence reviewed below.

## Full-paper evidence

- The paper describes a glass optical bridge with laser-written waveguides that attaches to a PIC at die level, allowing the optical subassembly to be electrically and optically tested before final package commitment.
- The prototype uses a 12-channel glass bridge and a blind-mate detachable fibre-array-unit connector. The connector includes a ferrule, alignment features, latch springs and a receptacle attached to the package.
- Average fibre-to-PIC coupling loss is reported at 1.41 dB and average detachable connector loss at 0.33 dB.
- Six preliminary repeated insertions show average absolute loss variation of 0.17 dB. The paper explicitly describes this as preliminary, not a lifetime qualification result.
- The latch-spring simulation estimates approximately 1.5 N mating force and approximately 910 MPa maximum stress; high-strength steel near a 1,000-MPa yield stress is proposed as a candidate.

## Evidence boundary

This is strong evidence for a known-good optical-subassembly and serviceable fibre-interface concept. It is not production yield or field-service evidence. The paper does not disclose lot-level attach yield, automated cycle time, environmental/lifetime qualification, field returns, ASP or margin. Claims are recorded as `CLM-495`–`CLM-499`.
