# PAP-029 — Li et al., FOWLP silicon-photonic engine

**Citation:** Xin Li et al., “1.6 Tbps (224 Gbps/λ) Silicon Photonic Engine Fabricated with Advanced Electronic-Photonic FOWLP for Co-Packaged Optics and Linear Drive Applications,” OFC 2024, Tu3A.2. DOI [10.1364/OFC.2024.Tu3A.2](https://doi.org/10.1364/OFC.2024.Tu3A.2). [Publisher record](https://opg.optica.org/abstract.cfm?uri=ofc-2024-Tu3A.2)

**Archive note:** the same-ID local `.html` file is a retained Optica/Radware CAPTCHA access attempt, not article text. The full PDF is retained separately and is the evidence reviewed below.

## Full-paper evidence

- The three-page conference paper reports a 1.6-Tb/s, eight-channel, 224-Gb/s/λ silicon-photonic engine fabricated with electronic-photonic FOWLP and positions the route as low-cost, volume-manufacturable and scalable.
- The package uses known-good dies in wafer reconstitution, through-mold vias, front-side RDL and backside RDL. The reported 60-Ω RF interconnect has less than 0.5 dB S21 loss at 50 GHz.
- The paper reports 112-Gb/s NRZ and 224-Gb/s PAM4 optical/electrical eyes. A 31-tap FFE equalizer gives PAM4 TDECQ of 2.08 dB and ER of 4.066 dB in the stated reference transmitter measurement.
- The optical engine is tested through a 1,000-µm front-side RDL RF transmission line; the paper presents the result as a CPO/LPO engine demonstration.

## Evidence boundary

The full paper strengthens the FOWLP cost/yield countercase, but its “low-cost” and “volume-manufacturable” language is an author framing, not measured production economics. It does not disclose lot-level good-engine yield, mould/edge-coupler defect rate, cycle time, rework, qualification, customer deployment, ASP or margin. Claims are recorded as `CLM-500`–`CLM-503`.
