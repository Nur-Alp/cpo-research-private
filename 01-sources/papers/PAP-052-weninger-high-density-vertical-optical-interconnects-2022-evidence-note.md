# PAP-052 evidence note — High Density Vertical Optical Interconnects for Passive Assembly

**Citation:** Drew Weninger, Samuel Serna, Achint Jain, Lionel Kimerling and Anuradha Agarwal, “High Density Vertical Optical Interconnects for Passive Assembly,” arXiv:2206.09125 (18 June 2022), 16 pages. [Canonical arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09125) · [local PDF](PAP-052-weninger-high-density-vertical-optical-interconnects-2022.pdf)

## What the paper measures or models

- The paper proposes a CMOS-compatible vertical evanescent coupler between 220-nm silicon and 220-nm silicon-nitride-on-glass waveguides for dense CPO assembly.
- Its simulation reports insertion loss below 0.13 dB at 1 μm inter-chip spacing, approximately ±2.7 μm 1-dB vertical and lateral alignment tolerance, greater than 300 nm 1-dB bandwidth, and approximately 2.3° twist and 0.4° tilt 1-dB tolerance (abstract; pp. 1–3).
- The proposed package uses optical fanout, passive pick-and-place/self-alignment and copper micro-pillar or C4/C2 bump assembly to reduce active alignment requirements (pp. 2–4).
- The paper argues that package scaling becomes constrained by optical-transceiver footprint and that vertical stacking can increase optical-I/O density, but these are architectural arguments rather than a demonstrated production package (pp. 1–3).

## Decision relevance

This is useful for the PIC/packaging control-point thesis: passive vertical coupling could reduce active-alignment time and enable denser optical engines. It does not establish a production yield, cycle time, Cpk, thermal qualification, field service procedure, complete engine power, BER/TDECQ or commercial cost. The coupler result is simulation-led and should not be treated as measured factory performance.

## Evidence boundary

Use PAP-052 to generate a manufacturing question—whether passive assembly and alignment tolerance can lower cost and yield risk—not as evidence that TSV/TGV, FOWLP, or any supplier has won. The paper predates current 200G/400G lane-rate and customer-qualification boundaries.
