# PAP-047 — Yi and Wilkerson, 3D optoelectronics and CPO deployment

## Citation

Yasha Yi and Danny Wilkerson, “3D optoelectronics and co-packaged optics: when solving the wrong problems stalls deployment,” arXiv:2603.21313 (2026). [ArXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21313) · [PDF](PAP-047-yi-wilkerson-3d-optoelectronics-cpo-deployment-2026.pdf).

## What was reviewed

The full 19-page PDF was retrieved and read on 2026-08-09. It is an open-access perspective/preprint, not a production or customer dataset.

## Bounded evidence

- The authors frame CPO and 3D photonic integration as system-level architectural commitments rather than component upgrades (pp. 1–6).
- They identify packaging, thermal gradients, yield compounding, standardization, serviceability, modularity and lifecycle robustness as adoption constraints (pp. 8–16).
- The paper explicitly argues that robustness per watt and total system behavior can matter more than a small isolated pJ/bit improvement (pp. 2–3, 15, 17).
- It connects integration density with compounded yield and deployment-risk concerns (pp. 9–10), but does not quantify a production yield or cost distribution.

## Evidence boundary

PAP-047 supplies a critical framework and falsification lens. It does not establish customer qualification, final-engine yield, field reliability, ASP, margin, adoption share or supplier profit. Claims are recorded as `CLM-448`–`CLM-450`.

## Research use

Use this preprint to prevent a power-only CPO thesis and to structure interviews around thermal-aware co-design, service boundaries, standardization and lifecycle/TCO. Pair it with measured records such as OIF fibre-count sensitivity, imec coupler yield, IBM reliability testing and PAP-046 TGV integration.
