- **Publication:** *Journal of Lightwave Technology* 43(10), 4903–4912 (2025)
- **DOI:** [10.1109/JLT.2025.3543339](https://doi.org/10.1109/JLT.2025.3543339)
- **Canonical publisher page:** [Optica record](https://opg.optica.org/jlt/abstract.cfm?uri=jlt-43-10-4903)
- **Local full text:** [`PAP-030-suda-polymer-waveguide-reliability-2025.pdf`](PAP-030-suda-polymer-waveguide-reliability-2025.pdf)

## Full-text evidence reviewed

The paper evaluates 11-mm single-mode polymer waveguides on FR4/glass-epoxy substrates for external-laser-source CPO. Eight fabricated waveguides had core dimensions of approximately 8.7 ± 0.2 µm by 6.9 ± 0.2 µm. At 1310 nm, insertion loss was −1.31 ± 0.16 dB (TE) and −1.24 ± 0.15 dB (TM); PDL was below 0.50 dB across the O-band and below 0.25 dB at 1310 nm. DGD was below 0.2 ps across the O-band. PER exceeded 20 dB for all eight waveguides at 1271, 1291, 1311 and 1331 nm.

For the high-power test, four waveguides were selected and each CWDM4 wavelength was assigned to a worst-case-loss sample. Input was set to +20 dBm at each waveguide facet (1271 nm was limited to +19.4 dBm); output variation remained within ±0.3 dB over six hours, with no excess loss or measurable power degradation. Thermal imaging at 1311 nm showed a 4.4 °C temperature rise after one hour at +20 dBm, close to the 4.9 °C simulated value.

## Interpretation boundary

This is strong component-level evidence that an external-light polymer redistribution path can tolerate high CW optical power under a short laboratory test. It does **not** establish accelerated-life qualification, production yield, fibre-attach loss distribution, automated alignment, complete-engine BER, customer acceptance, cost, ASP or margin. The authors identify longer reliability testing, PMF-array assembly and silicon-photonic integration as future work. Claims are recorded as `CLM-509`–`CLM-513`.
