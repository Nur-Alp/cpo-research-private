## What the release directly says

Broadcom states that Meta test results confirmed **one million cumulative
400G-equivalent CPO port device-hours of flap-free operation at Meta**. It
describes the setting as a high-temperature lab characterisation environment
and cites the ECOC 2025 paper *Co-Packaged Optics Technology Evaluation for
Hyperscale Data Center Fabric Switches* by Siamak Amiralizadeh et al.

The release also states Broadcom's comparison claim of 65% lower optics power
than pluggable modules and describes system mechanisms including thermal
management, monitoring, firmware/diagnostics and end-to-end electrical,
optical and mechanical validation.

## Evidence boundary

This is **named-customer historical reliability evidence at Meta**, but it is
not a Meta procurement, accepted-production-unit, installed-base, revenue,
repeat-shipment or field-service record. “Device hours” is not a switch count
or port inventory without a test duration and population. The release does
not supply the test population, switch SKU, configuration, thermal profile,
elapsed time, censoring, confidence interval, failure taxonomy, field-return
rate, warranty cost, optical-engine yield, supplier allocation, ASP or margin.

The accompanying ECOC title and Broadcom's own TH6 briefing identify the
detailed performance context as historical **TH5-Bailly / 100G-lane** CPO, not
TH6-Davisson 200G/lane. It therefore cannot clear the TH6 commercial-proof
gate or be transferred to NVIDIA Spectrum-X.

## Decision use

Use as a bounded historical control: Meta is a named lab-evaluation
environment for a stated cumulative device-hour result. It upgrades the
historical test-location/customer identity relative to an unnamed slide claim,
but does not establish a current 200G/lane deployment, volume leader,
profit-pool leader or public-equity conclusion.
