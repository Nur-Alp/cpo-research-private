# SKU-bound customer search audit — 11 August 2026

**Status:** Private retrieval log; a documented negative result is not proof that a non-public deployment does not exist.  
**Question:** Does a publicly accessible primary customer, OEM or integrator record identify a deployed NVIDIA `SN6810`/`SN6800` CPO configuration or Broadcom TH6-Davisson/`BCM78919`, with accepted units or ports and repeat shipment?  
**Decision use:** Commercial-proof gate only. This audit does not estimate market share, revenue or profit.

## Protocol

The search was deliberately SKU-first. It required all of the following before treating a result as switch-CPO deployment evidence:

1. an exact CPO product/configuration—not Spectrum-X, Tomahawk, 1.6T, “AI networking” or a different CPO generation;
2. a named customer/operator or a named OEM-integrator customer configuration;
3. acceptance/deployment rather than product availability, sample access, validation or a partnership quotation;
4. a dated unit, port, system or capacity denominator; and
5. a subsequent delivery, expansion, renewal or other repeatability record.

## Outcome

The 12 August retrieval details are preserved in the [commercial-proof retrieval addendum](commercial-proof-retrieval-addendum-2026-08-12.md). It adds no new exact-SKU customer numerator and does not change any commercial-proof gate.

The public record has a clear distinction between **orderable/limited-release products and verified deployments**:

```text
NVIDIA SN6810/SN6800: product + OEM route + select-partner shipment status
Broadcom BCM78919: product + early-access/limited-release status
Required but absent for both: named customer + accepted units/ports + repeat shipment
```

No current result clears the commercial-proof gate. In particular, Dell’s “Contact Sales” listing is not a customer order; Broadcom “Limited Release” is not a volume denominator; and Foxconn’s outlook is not observed CPO shipment. These controls should remain in force until a customer, procurement, deployment, financial, service or manufacturing record ties all required fields to the *same exact SKU*.

The 12 August Lambda result reinforces the architecture control. A named customer’s hands-on CPO sample can be real evidence for a **different** product boundary while remaining inadmissible for the Spectrum-X Ethernet commercial numerator. The system, fabric and SKU must remain matched before customer evidence transfers.

The Dell article is a useful false-positive control: it places a named customer quotation and an `SN6800-LD` image on the same page, but its text does not connect the two. A report that treated this as a named CPO deployment would be making an unsupported association.

The current first-party product pages improve product-boundary and timing confidence only. NVIDIA's second-half-2026 availability language is a vendor availability statement, not proof of accepted customer volume. Broadcom's live **Limited Release** status and absence of distributor inventory are consistent with an early commercial phase, not evidence of zero shipments; they simply leave the customer denominator unresolved.

The latest exact-string search adds two interpretation controls. First, NVIDIA's current full-production language and its second-half-2026 product-table availability should be treated as separate lifecycle signals: production readiness and customer availability do not equal accepted fleet volume. Second, Broadcom's phrase “foundation of CPO innovation and field shipments” refers to the historical CPO platform foundation; it is not evidence that TH6-Davisson/`BCM78919` has a named customer, accepted units or repeat shipments. Neither wording clears the same-SKU commercial numerator.

The TH6 OEM refresh reinforces the same control. A TH6-based switch that is
available to initial customers can still be a pluggable or mixed-interconnect
platform. Unless the source names the `BCM78919`/TH6-Davisson CPO
configuration, it cannot be used as a TH6 CPO customer numerator.

The Lambda refresh reinforces the opposite boundary: exact SKU and hands-on
operator evidence can exist without being admissible for the target thesis.
`Q3450-LD` is Quantum-X InfiniBand; the unresolved customer question is still
Spectrum-X Ethernet `SN6810`/`SN6800` or Broadcom `BCM78919`/TH6-Davisson.

The 13 August contract-manufacturer refresh adds an important timing control.
Hon Hai's public Q1 FY26 materials are forward-looking: they say CPO-switch mass
production and shipment are expected in Q3 2026, not that an exact NVIDIA or
Broadcom SKU has been accepted by a named customer. A generic shipment outlook,
even if subsequently echoed by media reports, is not an admissible exact-SKU
commercial numerator.
