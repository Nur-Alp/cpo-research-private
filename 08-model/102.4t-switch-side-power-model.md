# 102.4T Switch-Side Optical Power Model

**Owner:** Nur Alpys

**Status:** Preliminary scenario model, not measured system data

**As of:** 2026-08-07

## Decision question

For a 102.4 Tb/s Ethernet switch with 64 x 1.6T optical ports, how much switch-side optical-interface power could CPO, linear pluggable optics, and fully retimed pluggable optics require under a consistent calculation boundary?

## Answer in one paragraph

The central scenario produces approximately **601 W for CPO, 668 W for LPO, and 2,004 W for fully retimed optics** after illustrative conversion and incremental cooling overhead. These are model outputs, not measured 102.4T chassis results. The strongest conclusion is that eliminating receive-and-transmit DSP retiming could remove roughly two-thirds of switch-side optical-interface facility power. The modeled CPO advantage over LPO is much narrower at approximately 10% in the central case and ranges from approximately 2% to 20% across the scenarios. Public evidence is therefore insufficient to claim that power alone makes CPO commercially preferable to LPO.

## Model boundary

| Item | Boundary |
|---|---|
| Deployment | AI back-end Ethernet switch |
| Aggregate switch bandwidth | 102.4 Tb/s |
| Port configuration | 64 x 1.6T |
| Lane rate | 8 x 200G per 1.6T port |
| Reach | Up to 500 m comparison case |
| Utilisation | Full rated throughput |
| Included | Switch-side optical engines or modules, their lasers where the source includes them, power-conversion loss, and incremental cooling attributed to the optical interface |
| Excluded | Remote endpoint optics, switch ASIC core, CPU, memory, base fans, and unrelated chassis power |
| Reason for exclusions | No matched public 102.4T chassis disclosure was found across the three architectures |

The remote endpoint is excluded because it is assumed to be identical for each switch-side architecture. Switch-ASIC and host-SerDes power must be added later when comparable public or primary-research data becomes available.

## Source anchors

1. OIF reports a 5 pJ/bit target for back-end, compute, and other energy-sensitive interfaces, with some operators requesting 3 pJ/bit.[S1]
2. Broadcom claims TH6-Davisson reduces optical-interconnect power by 70% and is more than 3.5 times lower power than traditional pluggables.[S2]
3. Marvell reports that its 1.6T LPO light engine consumes less than 5 pJ/bit, including laser power, under typical operating conditions.[S3]
4. Marvell reports 1.6T retimed-module demonstrations at 22 W and 24 W.[S4][S5]
5. The OSFP1600 mechanical specification permits a module power envelope above 40 W. This is a connector and thermal envelope, not expected operating power.[S6]

Every numeric input below is either directly sourced, derived from a company claim, or labelled as an analyst scenario assumption.

## Formulas

For architecture (a) and scenario (s):

\[
P_{direct,a,s}
=
64 \times P_{port,a,s}
\]

\[
E_{direct,a,s}
=
\frac{P_{direct,a,s}}{102.4\ \text{Tb/s}}
\quad \text{pJ/bit}
\]

\[
P_{facility,a,s}
=
\frac{P_{direct,a,s}}{1-L_{conversion,s}}
\times
(1+C_{cooling,s})
\]

\[
E_{facility,a,s}
=
\frac{P_{facility,a,s}}{102.4\ \text{Tb/s}}
\quad \text{pJ/bit}
\]

One watt per terabit per second equals one picojoule per bit.

## Relative result and break-even

| Comparison | Optimistic | Central | Stress |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CPO facility-power saving versus fully retimed | 71.4% | 70.0% | 73.3% |
| LPO facility-power saving versus fully retimed | 70.9% | 66.7% | 66.7% |
| CPO facility-power saving versus LPO | 1.8% | 10.0% | 20.0% |

In the central case, CPO has only **0.8 W per 1.6T port**, **51.2 W direct per switch**, or **66.8 W facility-adjusted per switch** of modeled headroom over LPO. Any CPO-only laser-delivery, control, thermal, or service overhead omitted from the available source boundary that exceeds this amount would eliminate the modeled power advantage over LPO.

By contrast, CPO has **16.8 W per port** and approximately **1,402.5 W facility-adjusted per switch** of modeled headroom over the fully retimed case. The CPO-versus-retimed conclusion is therefore less sensitive to small boundary errors than the CPO-versus-LPO conclusion.

## Interpretation

### Academic LPO boundary check

The LPO paper set supports the direction of the model—removing full module retiming can reduce optical-interface power—but it does not justify replacing any central scenario input. Meta reports a 100G/lane experimental comparison of 20 pJ/bit for a fully retimed module against 12.5 pJ/bit for LPO, but its 200G result is modeled and its cited 5-10 pJ/bit CPO range is not a matched measurement.[S7] Semtech's stated less-than-10 W 1.6T LPO figure is an internal simulation plus estimates for laser and ancillary power, not a measured module.[S9]

The same literature indicates that the power outcome cannot be separated from the electrical-loss boundary. Meta's 200G analysis needs roughly 22-23 dB for its linear-drive limits, while Hisense and Semtech model different 31 dB and 26 dB cases with different assumptions.[S7][S8][S9] The 400G paper directly measures 160/180 GBd components but models 212.5 GBd only to 12 dB B2B loss, a direction consistent with NPO/CPO rather than established traditional LPO.[S10] These results are retained as architecture stress tests, not model inputs.

### What is supported

- **INFERENCE:** Removing full retiming has a potentially large optical-interface power benefit at 1.6T.
- **INFERENCE:** CPO and LPO could occupy a similar power range when both use linear electro-optics.
- **INFERENCE:** The commercial choice between CPO and LPO is likely to depend more heavily on density, link margin, reliability, serviceability, and sourcing than the CPO-versus-retimed choice.

### What is not supported

- This model does not prove actual TH6-Davisson chassis power.
- It does not prove Arista's demonstrated LPO system operates at the Marvell light-engine power level.
- It does not include measured ASIC, SerDes, fan, cooling, or power-supply data from matched systems.
- It does not prove total cost of ownership or commercial superiority.

## Next evidence required

1. Broadcom TH6-Davisson optical-engine and external-laser power measured at a stated boundary
2. Arista or module-vendor 102.4T LPO and fully retimed module power at equal reach and BER
3. Matched switch-ASIC and host-SerDes power by architecture
4. Chassis inlet power under a common workload and ambient temperature
5. Fan curves or cooling power attributable to faceplate and package thermal density
6. Optical attach rate, because partially populated systems change the CPO-versus-pluggable result
7. Qualification, failure-rate, and repair data to translate watts into total operating economics
